Building Platforms Cut for Geelong Blocks

Geelong has blocks of every shape. The older parts of the city step down toward Corio Bay and the Barwon River, so homes in suburbs like Highton, Belmont and Newtown are often built on genuine slopes, while the newer estates to the south are mostly flatter lots that still carry a fall and a layer of topsoil. Either way, nothing gets built until the site is cut to a level, stable platform that matches the engineering.

A site cut from Select Civil Group starts with the levels on your engineer's plans. We strip the topsoil, bring the high side down with laser levels guiding the machine, use that material to build up the low side where the design allows, and compact the platform to the standard the engineer has set. Surplus spoil is carted away, so the builder or slab crew arrives to a clean pad rather than a mound of soil to work around.

Where the fall is too steep to simply cut and fill, the platform usually needs a retaining wall on one or more sides. We build those as well, so the cut and the wall are planned together instead of being passed between two contractors. Andrew is on site for the dig and the final level check, so it is always clear who is responsible for the result.

What a Site Cut Includes

Every site cut we quote covers the full platform, not just the digging. Here is what is in the scope when we price a Geelong job.

In every site cut

  • Topsoil stripping - the organic layer comes off first, because it cannot be compacted to carry a slab.
  • Cut to engineered levels - laser levels on the machine so the platform finishes at the height shown on the drawings.
  • Cut and fill balancing - material from the high side reused on the low side where the design allows, cutting down on trucks and tip fees.
  • Compaction - the finished platform compacted to the standard your engineer specifies.
  • Spoil removal - whatever is left over is loaded out, carted away and disposed of legally.

Footings, service trenches and stormwater lines can follow on with our detail excavation, and once the pad is ready it is set for a concrete raft slab.

Every Kind of Geelong Block

No two cuts are the same. Here is how the job changes with the type of site.

Sloping blocks

Found through the hillier established suburbs. Deeper cuts, more spoil, and often a retaining wall to hold the platform.

New estate lots

Usually a gentler fall, but the topsoil still has to come off and the pad has to sit at the level the builder needs.

Extensions

Cutting beside an existing house, where access is narrower and the new platform has to suit the original building.

Sheds and outbuildings

Pads for sheds and outbuildings on house blocks and rural-residential properties, sized to the slab going on them.

Commercial pads

Larger platforms for commercial buildings, cut and compacted to the engineering specification.

Rock and hard ground

Where rock sits close to the surface, excavation takes longer, and that is allowed for in the price once we know it is there.

How a Geelong Site Cut Runs

From your engineer's levels to a pad ready for footings, in six steps.

Step 01

Levels and Photos

Send the address, the engineer's levels or site plan, and a few photos of the block from different angles.

Step 02

Look at the Block

We check the fall, access for the machine and trucks, and where the spoil is going to go.

Step 03

Written Quote

Itemised and in writing, with the cut, fill, compaction and cartage shown separately.

Step 04

Strip the Topsoil

The organic layer comes off first so the platform is built on ground that will compact.

Step 05

Cut to Level

The high side is cut with laser levels, fill is balanced across the block where the ground allows, then compacted.

Step 06

Pad Handover

Levels checked against the plans, spoil gone, and the platform ready for footings, detail excavation or the slab.
