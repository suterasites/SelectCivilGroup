Drysdale Driveways on Falling Ground

The Bellarine rises toward its middle and Drysdale is built across that rise, so plenty of blocks here fall toward the street or away from it. That shapes a driveway more than any choice of finish. A driveway climbing to a garage needs a grade a car can manage and a surface that grips on a wet morning, while one running down to the house needs the water caught and taken away before it reaches the garage door.

The town has grown in every direction, with new estates around Drysdale and Clifton Springs, infill through the established streets and larger semi-rural lots on the outskirts. Under most of it sits clay that moves through the seasons, so whatever the slope, the base is dug to depth and compacted properly before the steel goes in.

We are based in Geelong and on the Bellarine every week. If the block needs excavation or a retaining wall beside the driveway, they can be programmed into the same job, and the Drysdale civil page covers the rest of the site work.

Getting a Sloping Driveway Right

On a Drysdale block the slope decides most of the driveway, and these are the three situations it usually creates.

Driveways that climb

A steep driveway wants a surface with grip, so exposed aggregate or a heavier broom texture beats a smooth finish. The changes in grade at the street and at the garage are shaped so a car does not scrape on the way in or out.

Driveways that fall to the house

Water running toward the garage has to be stopped before it gets there, usually with a grated drain across the driveway connected to the property's stormwater. That is set out with the falls before the pour, not added later.

Walls beside the driveway

On a cut block the driveway often runs alongside a change in level, so the retaining wall goes in first and the driveway is formed against it. Doing both in one job means the levels line up and nobody has to come back to fix the join.

Choosing a Finish for Your Driveway

The finish is the biggest choice you make on a driveway. It changes the look from the street, the grip underfoot and the final price, so it is worth knowing how the options compare before you ask for a quote.

Driveway finishes we pour

  • Plain broom - a light broom texture across the surface for grip. The most affordable option and the one most Geelong driveways end up with.
  • Exposed aggregate - the surface is washed back to reveal the stone through the mix. More texture and grip than plain, at a higher price.
  • Coloured - oxide mixed through the concrete so the driveway suits the house, fence or render. Usually sits between plain and exposed on cost.
  • Stencilled or stamped - a pattern pressed or stencilled into the surface for a brick, slate or tile look. Priced at the higher end, alongside exposed aggregate.

Not sure which suits? Tell us about the property and how the driveway gets used, and we will talk the options through on site. Paths and outdoor areas can be poured to match with our concrete paving.

What Goes Into a Driveway Quote

Every quote we send is itemised, so you can see where the money goes. These are the things that move the number most on a Drysdale driveway.

Size and shape

Square metres set the concrete volume. Curves, a turning bay or a second parking space add formwork and labour.

Finish

Plain broom costs least. Exposed aggregate, coloured and patterned surfaces each add to the price.

Ground and base

Soft or reactive ground can need deeper excavation and more base material before the slab goes down.

Drainage

Where falls alone will not shift water off the driveway, drainage is built in before the pour.

Crossover

A new or widened crossover needs council approval and inspection, and it is priced in wherever it applies.

Access

A clear run for the concrete truck keeps costs down. Narrow side access or a long pump line adds time.

How a Drysdale Driveway Job Runs

Six stages in the same order every time, with a programmed start date locked in when you accept the quote.

Step 01

Measure-Up

Send the address and roughly what you have in mind. We check the size, the falls toward the street and the access for the truck before pricing.

Step 02

Itemised Quote

Finish, base, steel, drainage and any crossover work are listed as separate lines, so you can see what each choice adds.

Step 03

Excavation and Base

The ground is dug out to depth, then base material is spread and compacted to give the slab a firm, even bed.

Step 04

Formwork and Steel

Forms are set to the finished levels and falls, and the steel reinforcement is placed before the pour.

Step 05

Pour and Finish

Poured, screeded and finished in the surface you picked. On a standard residential driveway the pour is usually a one-day job.

Step 06

Handover

Forms stripped and the site cleaned up. Before we leave, we tell you how long to keep cars off the new concrete.
